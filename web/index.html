Executive Summary & Clinical Rationale

Solving the BMI Crisis with Privacy-Preserving Silhouette Anthropometry

Body Mass Index (BMI = kg/m²) has governed clinical screening for nearly two centuries, yet modern endocrinology recognizes it is fundamentally flawed. Body2Fit replaces coarse height/weight approximations with camera-derived volumetric anthropometry and automated reliability gating.

1. The Fatal Flaws of BMI

BMI cannot distinguish dense skeletal muscle from visceral fat. An athletic rugby player or bodybuilder is categorized as "Class 1 Obese," while a sedentary "TOFI" (Thin-Outside, Fat-Inside) patient with dangerous deep visceral adiposity passes as "Normal," missing early cardiovascular interventions.

2. Dual-View Anthropometry

Single-view photos lose the anteroposterior sagittal axis. Dual-view captures (frontal + lateral) reconstruct cross-sectional elliptical body slices. Extracting high-contrast binary silhouettes preserves absolute patient privacy—never storing or transmitting identifiable nude or semi-clothed imagery.

3. SMPL-X Reliability Gating

Unlike black-box models that silently hallucinate measurements when subjects wear baggy clothes or stand crooked, Body2Fit projects an anatomical SMPL-X mesh back into the silhouette camera. If render-back IoU drops below 65%, the system refuses to report risk, mandating recapture.

Clinical Indices & International Standards

Waist-to-Height Ratio (WHtR) WHtR = Waist (cm) / Height (cm)

Endorsed by the UK National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE 2022). A universal boundary of 0.50 applies across sexes and ethnicities. WHtR ≥ 0.50 flags early cardiometabolic risk; ≥ 0.60 indicates substantially increased risk.

Waist-to-Hip Ratio (WHR) WHR = Waist (cm) / Hip (cm)

WHO Expert Consultation benchmark. Distinguishes android (abdominal) from gynoid (peripheral) fat distribution. WHO thresholds: >0.90 for males and >0.85 for females correspond to substantial relative risk of Type 2 Diabetes.

Body Roundness Index (BRI) Thomas et al. (2013) Geometric Model

Models the human body as an eccentric ellipse. Proven in JAMA Network Open (2024) to predict all-cause mortality with a U-shaped hazard curve far more accurately than BMI.

InfoNCE Contrastive Multi-View Loss
L_info = -log( exp(sim(z_front, z_side)/τ) / ∑ exp(sim(z_front, z_neg)/τ) )

Aligns frontal and lateral representations into a cohesive subject identity representation while repelling negative sample pairs across the batch.

Anatomical Dimension Regression Loss
L_total = λ_reg · || y_pred - y_gt ||_1 + λ_contrast · L_info

Smooth L1 loss ensures robust convergence without outlier explosion, achieving millimeter-accurate tape measurement recovery.
